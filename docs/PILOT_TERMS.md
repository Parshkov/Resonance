# Resonance Pilot Terms and Acceptable Use

These pilot terms are intentionally short. They exist to make the consent/data boundary clear for competition judges and volunteer testers; they are not a substitute for production legal terms.

## Your content

You keep ownership of the ideas, Thought DNA, messages, notes, and artifacts you submit to Resonance.

By explicitly sharing a session for discovery, you give Resonance the limited permission needed to store, process, structurally compare, and return the **consented** parts of that session to provide the discovery service. Revoking sharing ends future live discoverability, subject to documented backup/audit retention.

By posting content to a private collaboration workspace, you allow Resonance to store and deliver that content to the workspace members who are authorized at the time of access.

## Consent boundaries

- Preparing a Thought DNA preview is not permission to publish it for discovery.
- Display profile, coarse location, and collaboration availability are separate consent choices where the product exposes them.
- Discovery does not grant permission to disclose private contact information.
- A workspace invitation must be accepted before private workspace content becomes visible.

## Acceptable use

Do not use the pilot to:

- upload content you do not have the right to share;
- seek unauthorized access to another user's private sessions, messages, workspaces, artifacts, or account;
- attempt to identify hidden users through aggregates/location data;
- submit malware, executable payloads, credential-stealing content, or abusive automated traffic;
- harass, spam, threaten, or repeatedly contact users who decline/block collaboration;
- intentionally use user-generated text to manipulate another user's agent into unauthorized actions.

The pilot may rate-limit, block, remove, or disable accounts/content that create security, abuse, or privacy risks.

## No guarantee of semantic correctness

Resonance is an experimental structural-discovery system. Matching, extraction, classification, evidence, and generated Thought DNA may be incomplete or wrong. Users should inspect the share preview and evidence rather than treating the system as an authority about another person's beliefs, identity, intent, expertise, or compatibility.

## Sensitive data

Do not submit secrets, passwords, authentication tokens, highly sensitive personal information, regulated confidential data, or information that would create material harm if exposed. The public pilot is designed for ideas and collaboration experiments, not regulated-data processing.

## Changes during the pilot

The product and these pilot terms may change before the competition release is frozen. The submitted judging release should pin the exact version of the code, privacy contract, and instructions used by judges.
